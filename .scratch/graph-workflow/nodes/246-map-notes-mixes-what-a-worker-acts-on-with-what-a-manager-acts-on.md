# `map.md`'s `## Notes` mixes what a worker acts on with what a manager acts on, and four workers have now reported it

Type: grilling
Status: superseded
Superseded by: 345

Blocked by: none

## Question

*Minted by the manager under this effort's own rule that **the second report of the same standing retrospective item
mints a node**. The question is the manager's; the evidence in it is four workers' text.*

`map.md` is read whole, cold, once per node by every worker, on the stated ground that it holds only what *every*
worker can act on. Four workers have now reported that most of `## Notes` fails that test for them, and the item has
been reported at least four times across two sessions.

[Node 144](144-make-the-rules-file-stand-alone.md)'s worker, quoted by node 241's worker, `verbatim`:

> `map.md`'s `## Notes` is about 5,000 words of which I acted on four items … while the standing preferences on
> concurrency, model heterogeneity, resume, token priority, worktree creation and one-question-at-a-time bore on
> nothing here.

[Node 241](241-two-record-defects-needing-a-decision-before-repair.md)'s worker, `verbatim`:

> `map.md` is the largest thing in my context and I acted on three items in it — never-delete and its two
> boundaries, the existence of the `Change the map` procedure, and the retrospective questions. The standing
> preferences on concurrency, model heterogeneity, resume, token priority, the gate payload's diff command, the
> grilling-node target and the payload trigger bore on nothing here. **This is at least the second report of this
> item**.

[Node 235](235-graph-conventions-says-a-node-resolves-at-its-gate.md)'s worker, `verbatim`:

> For a two-clause prose edit, the items that bore on nothing were the human-queue count and the one-question rule,
> the payload trigger, the no-diff-command rule, the manager-never-idles rule, the resume rule, the spent
> moved-standard licence, and the heterogeneous-harness note. That is most of `## Notes`. … **Several of those items
> are addressed to the manager alone** (the queue count, the payload trigger, the diff command, the never-idle rule,
> the resume rule) **and would sit in `map-manager-only.md` without a worker losing anything.**

[Node 166](166-ship-an-expedition-stop-skill.md)'s worker, `verbatim`:

> `map.md` items carry no marker for **who acts on them**. The effort has already moved manager-only and
> implementer-only material into siblings; what is left still mixes *what a worker does* with *what a manager does*,
> and a worker cannot tell which without reading each item whole. A one-word audience marker, or a further split,
> would let a worker skip about half the file. This is the same shape as the finding that produced
> `map-manager-only.md` and `map-implementation-rules.md`, one round further on.

**The two remedies the workers named, neither chosen:** a further split, moving the manager-only items out of
`map.md` into `map-manager-only.md` as [node 139](139-move-the-managers-material-out-of-the-map.md) already did once;
or a one-word audience marker on each item, leaving the file whole and letting a worker skip. They are not
equivalent — a split costs the manager a second file to keep true and saves a worker the read entirely, while a
marker costs one word per item and still ships the text into every worker's context.

**The counter-evidence, reported by the same worker that named the marker**, `verbatim`: *"Several of them I then
needed again as **subject matter** — because this node's product is a manager instruction — which is unusual and is
exactly why I cannot call them waste here; on any ordinary implementation node they would be."* So whatever is
decided has to survive a node whose subject is the manager's own conduct.
