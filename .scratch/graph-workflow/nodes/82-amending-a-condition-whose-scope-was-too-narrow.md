# How a condition's scope is amended once the extent turns out wider than it was written for

Type: grilling
Status: open

## Question

An acceptance condition fixes the scope of the work — *"these two files are edited, and nothing else is"* — and it
is written **before** anything has enumerated the defect it addresses. The enumeration arrives later, from the
**check**, by which time every route for changing the scope has closed: the worker may not rewrite the condition,
the checker may not, and the manager may not author one for a node another node's worker minted.

So when the extent turns out wider than the scope, **nothing on the graph can widen it**, and the case reaches the
human as a decision that mostly is not one.

### It has now happened twice in a row, at the same place

- **[Carry the worktree lifecycle into the shipped files](74-carry-the-worktree-lifecycle.md)** named two files.
  The lifecycle falsified statements in four. Flag `74.2` records it: *"both being outside the two files its
  condition named."* That overflow is why [Three `map.md` items nodes 64 and 68 left
  stale](80-map-items-nodes-64-and-68-left-stale.md) and [Bring the glossary's code-slot and reject entries up to
  the worktree lifecycle](81-glossary-code-slot-and-reject.md) exist.
- **Node 81 then did the same thing.** Its condition named `CONTEXT.md` *"and nothing else"*; the same checker's
  enumeration reached a fourth instance in `.claude/skills/expedition-prototype/SKILL.md`. The manager filed the
  problem and refused to repair it; the human widened it in four words, and the departure is flag `81.1`.

Neither case needed a judgement. Both were *the same defect, in a file the condition happened not to name*.

### What is at stake

**A scope clause is doing two jobs at once and only one of them is load-bearing.** It stops a worker wandering into
unrelated work, which is why it exists. It also, incidentally, freezes the defect's extent at whatever was known
when the condition was written — and that second effect is an accident of when the sentence gets written, not
something anybody decided.

**The routes that exist today, and what each costs.** Whoever claims the node **raises**, which stops the node and
spends a human turn. Or the overflow **becomes its own node**, which costs a node, a worker and a check to change
one line. Or the human amends it, as here, which works and leaves no rule behind.

**The thing that must not be lost.** A condition is the standard the work is judged against, and the reason the
manager may not author one for a worker-minted node is that authoring it makes the manager the author of that
standard. Any answer that lets a scope widen without a human has to say why widening *to the enumerated extent of a
defect the condition already names* is not that act — and where the line sits between widening a scope and
enlarging the work.

**One thing already on the record that bears on it.** [What a finding must carry so it can be
swept](71-what-a-finding-must-carry.md) makes a checker hand back the **extent** of every finding it reports. If
that lands, the enumeration exists at check time as a matter of contract rather than by the checker's own
initiative — which is what makes this question answerable at all, and also what will make it arise more often.
