# How a condition's scope is amended once the extent turns out wider than it was written for

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

An acceptance condition fixes the scope of the work — *"these two files are edited, and nothing else is"* — and it
is written **before** anything has enumerated the defect it addresses. The enumeration arrives later, from the
**check**, by which time every route for changing the scope has closed: the worker may not rewrite the condition,
the checker may not, and the manager may not author one for a node another node's worker minted.

So when the extent turns out wider than the scope, **nothing on the graph can widen it**, and the case reaches the
human as a decision that mostly is not one.

### It has now happened twice in a row, at the same place

- **[Carry the worktree lifecycle into the shipped files](74-carry-the-worktree-lifecycle.md)** named two files.
  The lifecycle falsified statements in four. Flag `74.2` records it: *"both being outside the two files its
  condition named."* That overflow is why [Three `map.md` items nodes 64 and 68 left
  stale](80-map-items-nodes-64-and-68-left-stale.md) and [Bring the glossary's code-slot and reject entries up to
  the worktree lifecycle](81-glossary-code-slot-and-reject.md) exist.
- **Node 81 then did the same thing.** Its condition named `CONTEXT.md` *"and nothing else"*; the same checker's
  enumeration reached a fourth instance in `.claude/skills/expedition-prototype/SKILL.md`. The manager filed the
  problem and refused to repair it; the human widened it in four words, and the departure is flag `81.1`.

Neither case needed a judgement. Both were *the same defect, in a file the condition happened not to name*.

### What is at stake

**A scope clause is doing two jobs at once and only one of them is load-bearing.** It stops a worker wandering into
unrelated work, which is why it exists. It also, incidentally, freezes the defect's extent at whatever was known
when the condition was written — and that second effect is an accident of when the sentence gets written, not
something anybody decided.

**The routes that exist today, and what each costs.** Whoever claims the node **raises**, which stops the node and
spends a human turn. Or the overflow **becomes its own node**, which costs a node, a worker and a check to change
one line. Or the human amends it, as here, which works and leaves no rule behind.

**The thing that must not be lost.** A condition is the standard the work is judged against, and the reason the
manager may not author one for a worker-minted node is that authoring it makes the manager the author of that
standard. Any answer that lets a scope widen without a human has to say why widening *to the enumerated extent of a
defect the condition already names* is not that act — and where the line sits between widening a scope and
enlarging the work.

**One thing already on the record that bears on it.** [What a finding must carry so it can be
swept](71-what-a-finding-must-carry.md) makes a checker hand back the **extent** of every finding it reports. If
that lands, the enumeration exists at check time as a matter of contract rather than by the checker's own
initiative — which is what makes this question answerable at all, and also what will make it arise more often.

## Answer

*Written by the node's worker and pasted as it wrote it.*

**The manager may widen a condition's scope without the human, under one test and two guards.** The human's words,
`verbatim`, on the recommendation as offered: *"ok for 82."* Everything under *The test* and *The two guards* is
theirs by that assent, the recommendation having been taken whole rather than amended.

### The test: the amendment adds places, never demands

The manager may extend a condition's scope to further instances of a defect the condition already describes,
**provided the amendment writes no requirement that was not already fixed** — by a resolved node, by what the shipped
files already say, or by text somebody else already wrote. Where saying what must be true at the new place needs a
judgement nobody has made, that is **enlarging the work**, and it is a raise or a node of its own.

**This is why widening is not authoring.** [Who writes the acceptance condition for an implementation node the
manager mints itself](39-condition-for-a-manager-minted-node.md) withheld the pen for a worker-minted node on one
stated ground: authoring a condition makes the manager the author of the standard the work is judged against. A
widening that adds no requirement does not do that — what makes an instance defective, and what a repaired instance
looks like, stays the minting author's words. The set of places it applies grew; the standard did not. That is the
line the node asked for, and it is the same limit node 39's constraint 1 already puts on a condition the manager
writes whole: *demand only what is already fixed*.

**Both amendments already on the record pass it, on their own recorded justifications.** Node 81's added entry 3
demanded node 74's already-shipped wording, *"copied across rather than composed here, which is why the amendment
decides nothing"*. Node 58's clauses 8–13 were **the node's own worker's text**, pasted from a condition that worker
had written for a companion node, with the manager renumbering and framing. So this legalises what has happened twice
rather than permitting something new.

### What the test does not reach

Three things are outside it, and naming them is what stops the permission being read as a general licence to edit a
condition.

1. **A new demand at any place.** A requirement nobody has fixed does not become fixed by being written into a
   condition. That is node 39's constraint 1 and it is unchanged here.
2. **An ambiguous demand at a place the condition already lists.** The scope is not short there — the *demand* is
   imprecise, and sharpening it is authoring the standard, which is exactly what widening is permitted because it is
   not. The live instance is node 58's clause 10, which required two glossary entries to *"state the same
   distinction"*: its worker read that as the distinction appearing in both from each subject's own side rather than
   as identical wording, and **flagged that a stricter reader would find it unmet**. That is the correct handling and
   it stays the existing route — the worker decides and flags, or raises where it cannot. The manager may not repair
   it under this permission.
3. **A defect the condition does not describe.** Reaching one is a new node, whatever file it lives in.

### The two guards

**Guard 1 — the enumeration is re-derived before the claim, not read.** Where a condition's scope is a list of files,
the manager re-runs the search that produced that list before the node is claimed. Where the list has grown, the
widening happens before any worker or checker has read the short version; where the growth fails the test above, it
reaches the human before a worker's time is spent rather than after.

This is [Qualify the graph-file ban in the worker contracts](58-qualify-the-graph-file-ban.md)'s own worker's
proposal, in its words *"a cheap, mechanical check"*, and that node's second round is the evidence for it. Re-running
the same grep after editing is what that worker did to confirm no flat statement remained, and it stated the lesson
generally: **"when a condition enumerates files, the first act is to re-derive the enumeration, not to read the files
it names."** It also reported that both human turns node 58 cost were about scope and *"both were answerable from the
repo before the first was asked."*

**Guard 2 — the amendment is recorded, marked, flagged and reported.** [Who writes the acceptance condition for an
implementation node the manager mints itself](39-condition-for-a-manager-minted-node.md)'s four constraints apply
unchanged, with the amendment in the condition's place: it is recorded on the node **beside the search that justified
it**, marked as the manager's own and distinguished from the minting author's text, carried as a flag, and named in
the report that accompanies the pick.

**Which report.** Guard 1 makes the claim the normal moment, so it is the **pick report**. Where an amendment is made
after the node is claimed — node 58's was, at a worker's raise — the amended text reaches that worker in the resume
that carries it, and the amendment is named in that node's closing report instead.

### Why this is not the manager taking a liberty the graph forbids

[Whether a spawn may override a shipped worker contract](28-spawn-overriding-a-contract.md)'s floor is untouched:
**nothing the manager sends a worker widens what that worker may write.** A scope clause bounds what the *work*
touches, not what the worker may write on the graph, and no widening here reaches the write rules.

And the amendment is not a **revision** in the glossary's sense — that word names an already-settled node turning out
wrong, handled in place or by supersession. A condition whose scope was short is neither: the node is unsettled, and
the glossary's **Acceptance condition** entry already routes a condition judged *wrong* to a raise. Widening is a
third thing, and *amendment* is the word both instances on the record already use for it.

### What this does not settle

- **Nothing independent checks the amendment.** Guard 2 is the manager auditing its own act, which is precisely flag
  `58.3`'s residual — *"nothing checked the amendment before it reached the worker"* — and it is the shipped manager
  contract's own unsettled duty 8, *"May you file a flag about your own conduct, and does anything sit between the
  judgement and the record?"* This answer does not close that hole; it puts the widening under the same self-audit
  every manager-written condition already gets, and leaves the hole where the contract already records it.
- **Whether a condition's scope should stop being an enumeration at all.** Guard 1 re-derives a list rather than
  removing the need for one. A condition could instead state the defect as a predicate over a named search domain —
  which is the shape [What a finding must carry so it can be swept](71-what-a-finding-must-carry.md) already makes a
  checker hand back, and which is self-contained text a checker can run against the repo. Not decided here, and
  reported as a node.
