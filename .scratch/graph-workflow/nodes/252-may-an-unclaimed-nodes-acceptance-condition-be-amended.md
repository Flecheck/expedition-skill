**ASK:** When a node resolves and makes a clause of an unclaimed node's acceptance condition wrong, may that clause
be repaired without spending one of your turns — and if so, who writes the replacement words?

**RECOMMENDED:** Yes — under one test, with the pen in a particular hand, and inside the guards that already exist.
Three parts, offered as one package.

**The test: every word of the replacement is already fixed, and the amendment says what fixes it.** Node 82's "adds

not merely extended, where every word of the replacement is fixed by a resolved node, by work already landed in the
tree, by the glossary, or by the shipped files — and the amendment names which. Where any part of the replacement
needs a judgement nobody has made, it is not amendable and it comes to you. That line is checkable by a reader,
which "adds places" was not.

**The pen belongs to whoever established the fact that broke the clause.** That party writes the replacement as text
on its handover, exactly as it already hands back flags and new nodes, and the manager files it — which is the
manager's ordinary scribe act and not an act of authorship. In all four instances above that worker had the repair
in hand, wrote it out, and stopped because nothing said it could. Where no such party exists, the manager writes it
under the test above. This is what makes the rule actually fire: the fix gets written at the moment it is cheapest,
by the only party holding both halves.

**Nothing else moves.** Node 82's guard 2 unchanged — recorded on the node beside what justified it, marked as its
author's and distinguished from the original author's text, carried as a flag, named in the report that goes with
the pick. Node 199's mechanics unchanged. And it reaches an **unclaimed** node only; once a worker holds it, your
raise rule governs and nothing changes.

What this rests on: four blocked nodes, none needing a judgement; the fact that the alternative instrument has
already broken in the same way; and node 199's own worker, which wrote that the manager *"will amend the next
condition without a rule to point at, and it is the human's own cut to extend."*

**ALTERNATIVES:**

- **Only you amend, as at node 166.** Proven and on the record. Costs one of your turns per instance — four owed

- **Keep the repair-node route (nodes 203, 245).** Buys independent eyes, but costs a node, a worker, a check and a

- **Change nothing.** Every falsified condition becomes a raise by whoever claims the node. That is today, and node
  208 measures what it costs: three workers trapped in three consecutive nodes, each paying to rediscover the same
