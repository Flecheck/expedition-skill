## Answer

*The worker's own text, pasted.*

**The question is re-rendered, not retired. Nothing sweeps it, because a sweep is the wrong instrument — what was
missing is a duty at the moment each report arrives.** The defect this node was told to work around turned out to be
in the manager's wording of the question, not in the question the human asked for.

### 1. The correction that reshaped this node

The manager's rendering — *what did you read and did not use* — put the worker's own reading at the centre, so its
answers came back as citation counts, and [node 129](129-what-to-do-about-the-records-token-cost.md) was right that a
citation count establishes nothing about non-use. From that I recommended deleting the question. **That was wrong,
and the human said why.** **The subject is what was *fed* — the spawn's payload and the reads the contract makes
mandatory — and, second, bloat inside a read that was itself necessary.** Neither is a citation count. A worker did
not choose the fed material and has no stake in justifying it; and a worker that went to a file for one fact and took
three hundred lines can say so exactly, because it knows what it went for.

### 2. The re-rendered question

The map's retrospective item keeps four questions. The second becomes, in full:

> **What arrived in your context that this node did not need.** A judgement made backwards, from the work you
> finished to the context you started with — not a list of what you did not quote, since material you never cited may
> still have kept you from re-asking something or from contradicting it. Three places to look: what your **spawn**
> fed you that bore on nothing; what your **mandatory reads** hold that this node had no use for; and, where you went
> to a file for one fact and took the whole file, what you went for set against what came with it. Where a different
> arrangement would have avoided it — a heading, a pointer, a narrower search — say so, because that is the answer
> this question is for.

Three properties are deliberate. It **states its own epistemics**, so no worker produces the count again. It
**separates fed from fetched**, which is the distinction the old wording collapsed. And it **names its actionable
output** — a structural fix — rather than a list.

### 3. The third question gains the record as a fourth place

*What would have made this node easier* today names *"the spawn, the node file, or the contract"*. The record is not
among them. **Two workers independently invented a fourth heading of their own, `In the graph:`** — node 136's and
node 138's — to report that nothing records `map.md`'s size over time. Node 139's worker reported it a third time. It
has been reported three times and acted on zero times, and one reason is that the question it answers has no slot for
it. So the third question reads *"in the spawn, the node file, the contract, **or the record**"*.

### 4. Nothing sweeps, and a sweep is the wrong instrument

**The failure this node was named for is real and the diagnosis in its title is wrong.** Nothing was owed at the
moment each report arrived, and a sweep would not have supplied it — a sweep reads the corpus late, in bulk, and
reading twenty-three of these answers in bulk is exactly what turns a pile of considered judgements into a number.

Two things stand in a sweep's place, both at arrival. **The manager disposes of each item that names the record, in
the report that closes the node** — it acts on it, mints a node for it, or says why not. And **on the *second* report
of the same standing item, the manager mints a node.** Weight here comes from repetition, and the record shows the
manager cannot see repetition unaided: node 113 exists because **a worker** counted to four. The map-size finding is
at three and unminted. Second, not fourth.

**No seventh graph file, and no tally.** Its weakness is stated rather than hidden: it depends on the manager
noticing, which is the faculty that failed. See flag `141.3`.

### 5. Node 60 is untouched, and the two are not two directions of one thing

Node 60 ships a `MISSING FROM MAP:` handover part on which a worker reports what **`map.md`** did not tell it. **One
procedure does not serve both**, and after [node 143](143-rules-arrive-by-the-spawn-never-by-a-search.md) they are
not even about the same object: node 143 moves the standards and the bar's commands out of a file the worker reads
and into what its **spawn** names. Node 60 is about a *file*; the re-rendered question is about a *payload*.

**Node 143 makes this question more load-bearing, not less.** As more of what a worker holds is composed by the
manager rather than read from a file, the worker becomes the only party who can tell the manager the payload was
wrong — and a payload defect is invisible from the manager's side, because the manager sees what it sent and not what
was needed.

### 6. The re-rendered question has already produced the human's own example, four times, and nothing swept it

The corpus holds four independent reports of exactly *"a rules file that had a high portion of information that
turned out to be unneeded"*, all about method skills the contracts then mandated — nodes 114, 100, 116 and 107.
**The site was fixed at node 113 and not by these.** Node 113's trigger was three unanswered flags about workers
*declining to invoke*, a different complaint that happened to reach the same lines. The four reports sat unswept, and
the fix arrived by a route that had nothing to do with them. **That is the arrival-duty failure, measured on the
human's own example.**

### 7. What my own evidence turns out to have been

**I made, on my own evidence, the error I was charging the record with.** My question told the human that seven of
twenty-three answers disclaimed the surfeit reading. I produced that by matching phrases and inferring a stance from
them — a citation count. Re-read against the corrected reading: **two are not disclaimers at all but affirmative
answers to the human's question**; **two report bloat within a necessary read and disclaim only blame**; three are
genuine and node-specific. **The corpus is full of the answers the human describes, and my framing discarded them.**
Eighteen of the twenty-three name material from `map.md` or `decisions.md` — both mandatory, both fed.

### 8. What node 129 established, and where this narrows it

**Its core stands untouched:** absence of citation is evidence of nothing. **Two of its statements are narrowed** —
the answers were never a metric, and the findings about **fed** material are judgements about a payload the worker
did not choose. **One consequence runs the other way and matters:** node 136's Bound 1 counted namings of `map.md`
sections, which are fed, so node 136 is substantially rehabilitated and nodes 139 and 140 do not rest on a broken
instrument. **What is still not evidence is the tally.**

### 9. The cost, unchanged

The read-and-unused answers are **34,666 bytes, 28.4% of all retrospective text on this effort**, mean **1,507 bytes**
per node across 23 answers. **The re-rendering does not reduce the cost and is not offered as a saving.** This is a
correctness finding: the question was producing an answer to a question nobody asked.
