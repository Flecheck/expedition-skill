# Whether a research node's and an AFK precondition node's flags settle at the pick report too

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

*Surfaced by [A node's flags arrive after the human moment that was supposed to settle
them](99-flags-arrive-after-the-node-s-human-moment.md)'s worker, and pasted as it wrote it.*

> This effort's shipped files route a `research` node's and an AFK `precondition` node's flags to the effort's
> closing report, on the stated ground that those kinds *"never end in front of the human"*. Node 99 establishes that
> ground is false: every node's close is reported by a pick report, those two kinds included, so they have exactly
> the moment they are said to lack. The question is whether they move to it. Moving them shrinks the closing report's
> flag list to almost nothing — flags raised before the practice existed, and flags whose pick report the human never
> answered — and it settles an AFK worker's judgements on a human word that was never about them. Not moving them
> means two kinds of node have a moment the workflow declines to use, and the closing report keeps a job the rest of
> the design has taken away from it. The answer also has to say what happens to `flags.md`'s stated purpose, which
> node 27 defended partly on those two kinds having nowhere else to go.

**Blockers, in the worker's words:** *"none."*

## Answer

*Written by this node's worker and pasted as it wrote it.*

**They move. A node's flags are settled at the first human moment its close reaches — an implementation node's gate
where it has one, and otherwise the pick report that closes it.** The human, on the recommendation: *"ok for
recommended."*

The rule no longer branches on the node's kind. There is no kind whose flags wait for the closing report because of
what kind it is.

### Why the exception had to go rather than be re-grounded

The shipped files routed a `research` node's and an AFK `precondition` node's flags to the effort's closing report on
the ground that those kinds *"never end in front of the human"*. [A node's flags arrive after the human moment that
was supposed to settle them](99-flags-arrive-after-the-node-s-human-moment.md) established that ground is false:
every node's close is reported by a **pick report**, those two included, so they have exactly the moment they were
said to lack.

Nothing else was ever offered in its place. The exception rested on that one claim and on nothing that survives it,
so re-grounding it would have meant inventing a reason after the fact for a rule whose only stated reason had failed.

Three things carried the decision beyond that. Judging a flag is cheapest while the work is still in front of
everybody, which is the whole ground of the settle-at-close practice the human instructed. On an effort whose
frontier never empties the closing report may never be written, so *"waits for the closing report"* can mean
*"reaches nobody"* — the residual [Whether flags.md survives the settle-at-close practice](27-does-flags-file-survive.md)
named and did not repair. And the one real objection — that an AFK worker's judgements are settled by a word of the
human's that was never about them — is already the accepted design for a grilling node's flags under node 99, and is
answered by the duty the pick report carries: a report that does not present a flag does not settle it.

### What the human's word reaches

The reply took the recommendation whole, including *"drop the distinction by node kind entirely"*. So the rule
reaches every kind, and two of them were never named in the question this node was asked:

- a **prototype** node, which has no gate and no exchange with the human, and which no shipped sentence ever routed
  anywhere — it was neither in the exception nor in the norm;
- a **precondition** node that is *not* AFK, having opened a raise mid-work. A raise is not a gate, so its close
  reaches the human at the pick report like any other.

That widening is recorded as flag `102.1` rather than assumed silently.

### What does not change

- **The implementation gate stays the settling moment for an implementation node.** Its flags exist before the gate,
  the gate payload carries them, and approval settles them. The gate is a real claimant with a real answer, so it is
  strictly better than the pick report that follows it.
- **Every clause node 99 attached to the pick report holds unchanged for these kinds too.** The report awaits no
  answer and takes no place in the human's queue; the settlement rides whatever the human says next, whatever that
  next thing is about; where the human never speaks again nothing settles and the flags reach the closing report by
  the existing route; the manager never settles a flag on its own judgement or on the passage of time.
- **The presentation duty holds, and it is what makes this safe.** A pick report closing a node that holds flags
  carries every one of them in the fuller form a gate payload uses — what was decided, why, and the context needed to
  answer without opening a file — because a payload that omits a flag settles it silently. The duty matters *more*
  here than for grilling: the human took part in a grilling node's interview and arrives at its pick report with
  context, while an AFK node's pick report is the first and only time they see that node at all.
- **`## What is in front of the human, and when` stands**, on the same ground node 99 left it on. Settling a flag is
  still not a claimant, because it still rides a moment that already exists.

### What the closing report is left with

Two populations, and neither is a node kind:

1. flags raised before the settle-at-close practice existed, whose nodes are already resolved and can never be
   settled at a close;
2. flags whose pick report the human never answered, because they never spoke again.

The kind-based third population is empty by this decision. The closing report keeps a job that is bounded and no
longer grows with the graph, which is the correct shape for an exception path.

### What happens to `flags.md`'s stated purpose

**Untouched.** Node 27 kept the file on three grounds, and this is the second of them — already annotated as false on
that node from node 99's `INVALIDATIONS`, and now removed by decision as well as by fact. Its other two are
unaffected: the outstanding flags whose nodes resolved before the practice existed, and a flag surviving its own
gate, of which `22.4` is the recorded instance.

The decision does not depend on the ground that failed, and node 27 said so itself: *"its value was never the state
column."* The file is the graph's capped index of every judgement an agent made on its own — the flag counterpart to
`decisions.md` — and what makes it worth having is the report-shaped one-line summary written at raise time by the
agent that held the context. Shrinking the unchecked list to almost nothing is what node 27 predicted and priced, not
a loss of its purpose. Settled by the worker rather than asked; recorded as flag `102.2`.

One instruction in the `precondition` contract survives the change of its ground: *"Write it short and plain enough to
be settled cold."* It gets **more** binding, not less, because the reader is now a human meeting that node for the
first time in a pick report.

### What node 103's condition does not yet cover

Reported as a finding, not done here — [Carry the pick-report settling moment into the shipped
files](103-carry-the-pick-report-settling-moment.md) is where the rewriting happens.

Its condition, written before this node was answered, says *"no file in the repository still says that a grilling
node's flags are settled at that node's last answer."* Under this answer three further things are true and its five
listed sites do not all reach them:

- `.claude/skills/expedition/graph-conventions.md`, `### Settle a flag` — its list of what the closing report carries
  names *"those on the kinds of node that never end in front of the human, `research` and an AFK `precondition`"*.
  That clause goes; the two surviving populations above replace it.
- `.claude/skills/expedition-precondition/SKILL.md` — *"This kind of node passes through no gate, so a flag here
  waits for the closing report."* **This is a site node 103's condition does not name at all**, and it carries a
  *different* false ground from the one node 99 falsified: no gate, rather than no human moment. Having no gate is now
  irrelevant to where a flag settles.
- `.claude/skills/expedition-research/SKILL.md` states no routing rule at all, so nothing there needs removing. This
  node's own question overstates the spread of the sentence for that kind.

Whether that is a widening node 103's condition may take under [How a condition's scope is
amended](82-amending-a-condition-whose-scope-was-too-narrow.md) is not this node's to decide: it adds a place *and*
changes the rule's coverage, where that procedure licenses adding places and no demand.
