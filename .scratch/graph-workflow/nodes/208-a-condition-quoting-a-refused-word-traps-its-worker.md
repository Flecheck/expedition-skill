An acceptance condition names the material it demands by quoting a phrase, and `CONTEXT.md` refuses one of the words
in that phrase for exactly the concept the condition means. The worker cannot ship the condition's word without
breaking the bar, and cannot be sure a checker reading the clause literally will accept the substitution — so it
substitutes, flags, and pays for the judgement. **This has now happened three times in three consecutive nodes.**

- [Node 196](196-give-the-rules-file-its-admission-test.md), flag `196.1`, on clause 1(b)'s *"what a run cost"*:
  *"`CONTEXT.md` refuses `run` as a synonym for both **effort** and **attempt**, and the glossary is authoritative
  over this effort's prose."*
- [Node 197](197-ship-what-a-file-of-the-map-admits.md), flag `197.1`, on clause 2's same phrase: *"the condition's
  author was writing wording for a file governed by a glossary that refuses the word for that very concept."*
- [Node 200](200-collapse-the-rules-files-two-map-history-statements.md), in its retrospective, on clause 3's
  *"retired"*: *"nothing warned that the condition's own word `retired` is refused by `CONTEXT.md` while the bar
  holds my prose answerable for it. A condition quoting vocabulary the bar refuses sets a small trap for its
  worker."*

Node 197's worker named the repair its own condition would have wanted: *"A condition for a shipped prose file is
cheaper to meet if it says **the material, in whatever words the file's glossary allows** rather than quoting
phrases."* Whether that is the rule, where it lives, and whether it binds the condition's author or its reader, is
what this node decides.
