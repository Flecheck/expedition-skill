# Whether an acceptance condition is a rule statement or a record, under the never-delete cut

Type: grilling
Status: claimed

Blocked by: none

## Question

*Surfaced by [State where the never-delete rule stops, in this effort's
map](193-state-where-the-never-delete-rule-stops-in-the-map.md)'s worker, and pasted as it wrote it.*

> An acceptance condition binds a live worker exactly as a rule does, and is quoted forever after as *"pasted as it
> wrote it"* exactly as a record is. **[Node 189](189-where-the-never-delete-rule-stops.md)'s two lists place it in
> neither** — its rule side names the ground rules, the four standing rules, `map.md`'s standing preferences and the
> landing policy's operative instructions; its record side names a node's `## Answer`, a line in `decisions.md`, an
> entry in `flags.md`, an `## Interview` attempt and a `## Review` round. **The practice and a literal reading of
> the rule now in `map.md` disagree.** The manager amended node 184's clause 2 and node 190's clause 3 by striking
> in place and writing the replacement underneath — node 190's within the hour of node 189 resolving, and expressly
> to match this rule — which is the record-side treatment; a literal reading of the preference this node just wrote
> says a superseded clause is *replaced* and its text banked verbatim to `map-history.md`. Which is it? The record
> leans record-side: a condition's job in the file it sits in is to record what was asked of that node before work
> started, and node 189's flag `189.3` draws the cut by exactly that job-in-its-file test. But nothing states it,
> the manager will amend the next condition without a rule to point at, and it is the human's own cut to extend.

**Blockers, in the worker's words:** *"none."*
